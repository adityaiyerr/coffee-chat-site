Five Pillars of Civilization:
Series Prologue
Aditya Iyer September 2026 Equity Research
Introduction

There are very few things one can attribute to modern civilization's ability to thrive. After reading Vaclav Smil's How the World Really Works: A Scientist's Guide to Our Past, Present, and Future, I was inspired to write this five-part series to expand further on what Smil describes as the four pillars of civilization (with an extra fifth pillar which I find to be most important of all). The four pillars are ammonia, steel, concrete, and plastics. Over the next few weeks, I'll give a brief overview of each pillar's importance, expand on relevant companies, examine their financial performance, and provide my investment opinion (where I see valuations heading). I'll then cover the fifth pillar, energy, in a larger, broader section, and how it ties into rising global energy demand driven by artificial intelligence and the quest for sustainable, renewable energy sources to empower a re-industrialization and complete overhaul of current power grids.
